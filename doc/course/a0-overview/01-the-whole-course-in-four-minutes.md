## On YouTube

**Title**  
Learn Takes for Blender: the whole course in four minutes

**Description**  
What you will be able to do, the four stages, the one product everything is built on, how to watch, and how your ideas steer where it goes next.

## What this video has to do

- Sell the course in the first thirty seconds, with the product doing the selling rather than the narration.
- Say what the four stages are and roughly how long each takes.
- Set the expectation that the add-on ships often, without making that sound like a warning.

## What the viewer already knows

- Possibly nothing at all. This is the channel trailer as well as the course opener.

## What they can do afterwards

- They know whether the course is for them and where to start.
- They know a video may show a button that has since moved, and that the page under each lesson is current.

## What to show, in order

1. The finished demo, working, no talking. Finishes, lighting, angles.
2. The syllabus page: four stages, times on screen.
3. Stage A named as the part that is enough to do real work.
4. The one product everything is built on.
5. How to watch: one problem per video, none longer than eight minutes.
6. The invitation — the problem you hit is the one worth fixing.

## Set up before you record

- The full demo file, finished.
- The syllabus page open, Stage A expanded.

## Where to be careful

- No feature names. This video teaches nothing.
- The 'ships often' beat is a reason to join, never an apology.
- Times on screen must match the syllabus. It is generated — read it, do not remember it.

## Out of scope

Name it and move on. Do not explain it here.

- Everything. Nothing is taught here.
